Incident Type: Grand Theft — Retail (PC 487)

NARRATIVE:
On April 18, 2024, at approximately 1422 hours, I, Officer P. Sullivan, responded to a report of a retail theft at Bay Area Electronics, located at 3200 Lakeshore Avenue, Oakland, CA. Upon arrival, I spoke with the store manager, Mr. Henry Yamamoto, who reported that a shoplifting incident had occurred approximately 15 minutes prior.

Mr. Yamamoto stated that at approximately 1405 hours, two individuals entered the store through the main entrance on Lakeshore Avenue. One individual, described as a white female approximately 25-30 years old with shoulder-length blonde hair wearing a green army-style jacket, engaged a sales associate (employee Kevin Marsh) in conversation about wireless headphones in the audio section near the front of the store. While the associate was occupied, the second individual, described as a white male approximately 30 years old with a shaved head and a goatee wearing a gray sweatshirt, moved to the high-value electronics display near the rear of the store.

Security camera footage reviewed on scene shows the male suspect removing three Apple iPhone 15 Pro Max units (retail value $1,199 each) and two Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra units (retail value $1,299 each) from the locked display case. The case was opened using a magnet key — Mr. Yamamoto suspects the key was stolen from the store during a prior visit. The suspect placed the items in a lined bag designed to defeat electronic article surveillance (EAS) tags.

The two individuals exited the store through the rear emergency exit onto Rand Avenue at approximately 1412 hours, triggering the door alarm. By the time staff reached the exit, the suspects had entered a silver SUV (possibly a Toyota RAV4, partial plate: 7M_____) and departed northbound on Rand Avenue.

Total estimated loss: $6,195 (three iPhones at $3,597 + two Samsung phones at $2,598).

This incident is the second significant theft from Bay Area Electronics in the past 30 days (see Case #2024-00298). The MO is similar: a two-person team using distraction and a magnet key. Mr. Yamamoto believes this may be an organized retail theft ring.
